Key Takeaways from the Data

August 2026

1. Youth and children are more involved in gun violence as victims than as perpetrators.

People under 18 are involved in gun violence far more often as victims than as perpetrators. Since 2017, 1.7 times as many youth and children have been victimized by gun violence as involved as perpetrators. This figure excludes victimless incidents that involve only possession of a firearm. At the height of the recent spike in gun violence, children and youth were twice as likely to be victims of gun violence than perpetrators. The experience of children and youth as victims of gun violence is one of the most overlooked and enduring trends in the GVA data.

Ratio of youth gun violence victims to perpetrators, by year, 2017 - 2025

2. The pandemic-era spike in gun violence was real, and youth were primarily impacted as victims.

Around the time of the pandemic, the youth gun victimization rate doubled, from about than 10 per 100k per year in February 2019 to more than 20 in June 2023 (measured by the rolling 12-month average). The rate at which youth were involved in gun violence as perpetrators also increased significantly during this time frame, but the magnitude was far smaller. Where youth victimization per 100,000 increased by 10 youth per year, perpetration rates increases by just 3 youth per 100,000 per year - nearly a 50% increase but a much smaller magnitude. According to the GVA data, the increase in youth victimization by firearms is the biggest story to tell about changing gun violence around the time of the pandemic. Notably, in the media and public imagination, this victimization story was often misconstrued as a story about youth violence being out of control.

Youth gun violence victims and perpetrators per year per 100,000 youth, by month, 2017 - 2026

3. The pandemic-era surge is over.

The pandemic-era surge in victimization and perpetration is over. Youth and child involvement in gun violence has fallen substantially from its peak — for victims and perpetrators alike. Children’s involvement in gun violence is down approximately 40%. Youth involvement in gun violence as victims is down by nearly one-third, and youth involvement in perpetrating gun violence is down 19%.

4. Youth violence is a small part of the picture compared to adults.

Of all the gun violence incidents recorded in the United States, the vast majority does not involve people under 18 at all. Across the full 2017–2026 period, about 85% of incidents involve only adults. Gun violence is overwhelmingly an adult phenomenon. This chart shows the total number of incidents involving adults alone versus people under age 18 as victims or perpetrators. The number of adult-alone incidents increased 60% over its pre-pandemic level, where children- or youth-involved incidents increased a relatively modest 34%.

Gun violence in the United States is a significant public safety problem and one that became especially acute in the early part of this decade. But before, during, and after the recent increase, the shape of gun violence in the United States has overwhelmingly been defined by incidents that involved adults alone.
