Key Takeaways from the Data

1. Youth and children are more involved in gun violence as victims than as perpetrators

The rate at which people under 18 experience gun violence as victims is persistently higher than the rate they are involved in gun violence as perpetrators. At the height of the recent spike in gun violence, youth and children were more than twice as likely to be victims of gun violence than perpetrators. The experience of children and youth as victims of gun violence is one of the most overlooked and enduring trends in the GVA data.

Youth and child gun violence victims and perpetrators per year per 100,000 people under 18, by month, 2017 - 2026

2. The pandemic-era spike in gun violence was real, but youth were primarily impacted as victims.

Around the time of the pandemic, the youth gun victimization rate doubled, from about than 10 per 100k per year in February 2019 to more than 20 in June 2023 (measured by the rolling 12-month average). The rate at which youth were involved in gun violence as perpetrators also increased significantly during this time frame, but the magnitude was far smaller. Where youth victimization per 100,000 increased by 10 youth per year, perpetration rates increases by just 3 youth per 100,000 per year - nearly a 50% increase but a much smaller magnitude. According to the GVA data, the increase in youth victimization by firearms is the biggest story to tell about changing gun violence around the time of the pandemic. Notably, in the media and public imagination, this victimization story was misconstrued as a story about youth violence being out of control.

3. The pandemic-era surge is over

The pandemic-era surge in victimization and perpetration is over. Youth and child involvement in gun violence has fallen substantially from its peak — for victims and perpetrators alike, in both age groups.

Whatever surge in youth gun violence the pandemic produced, it has substantially reversed — on both sides of the ledger, for both age groups.

4. Youth violence is a small part of the picture compared to adults

Of all the gun violence incidents recorded in the United States, the vast majority does not involve youth or children at all. Across the full 2017–2026 period, about 85% of incidents involve only adults. Gun violence is overwhelmingly an adult phenomenon.This chart shows the total number of incidents involving adults alone versus youth as victims or perpetrators. The number of adult-alone incidents increased 60% over its pre-pandemic level, where youth-involved incidents increased a relatively modest 34%.

Youth gun violence is a real problem, and the pandemic-era rise in youth victimization was real. But it was never the driver of the broader crisis, and it was never primarily a story about youth as offenders. It was, and remains, a story about kids getting caught in the crossfire of a much larger, adult-driven surge in American gun violence — one that, on every measure we can track, is now in retreat.
